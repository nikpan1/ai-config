Build a source-grounded business knowledge graph from the verified facts and original source spans in the payload. Return only JSON matching SemanticGraphDraft.

The graph preserves main topics, their rules, logical groups of conditions, and exceptions. Allocate every input fact exactly once to one rule using fact_ids. Never allocate facts seen only as context or in the existing topic register. Keep each rule scope identical to its assigned facts and its topic. Do not merge distinct systems, versions, audiences or incompatible scopes. Use separate topics when scope differs. A topic can have many rules, including contrary rules; never resolve a contradiction by dropping a rule.

Consult topic_register before proposing a topic. Reuse an existing topic's exact name and scope, and preferably its id as key, when the source establishes the same business topic. Otherwise create a concise canonical business name. Topic keys and rule keys identify objects within this response. Do not merge merely similar words or infer an unstated shared topic. Add explicit findings for ambiguous scope, unresolved references, or ungrounded topic relationships. Every topic must own a rule in this response.

Preserve verified facts' conditions and exceptions. Keep elementary condition statements and exception statements verbatim from the facts. If a fact condition combines multiple prerequisites with AND/OR, copy its exact phrase into condition_groups.source_conditions and decompose it into elementary member conditions with the matching all/any operator. source_conditions binds a complete verified condition to its decomposition; it is not another Condition node. Do not copy a compound AND/OR phrase into a single member just to satisfy string preservation. These statements may paraphrase the source; the separate quote.text must always be verbatim original text. Preserve negation, exclusions, numbers, temporal restrictions and actors. Do not turn necessary requirements into sufficient guarantees. Use modality necessary, sufficient, necessary_and_sufficient, descriptive, or prohibited only as supported. A condition group's operator is all for conjunction, any for alternatives, or unspecified if the source does not decide. Each group needs its own quotes supporting the logical combination. Its member conditions need their own quotes. Exceptions belong to their specific rule. Do not silently move an exception onto an unrelated condition.

Every topic, rule, condition group, condition and exception requires one or more exact evidence quotes. Each rule also requires topic_quotes that demonstrate the topic-to-rule relationship, not just separately mentioning the endpoints. Every assigned fact needs a rule quote from its evidence_ids. A quote is {evidence_id,start,end,text}; offsets are zero-based Python Unicode character indices within the original source.text, with end exclusive. The exact slice source.text[start:end] must equal quote.text. Preserve Markdown bytes, punctuation, spaces and line breaks. Select the actual occurrence by offsets when text repeats. Quote multiple source spans if the claim depends on more than one. Context spans can ground qualifications and links, but their facts must not be counted as newly owned.

The response is {topics:[{key,name,scope,quotes}],rules:[{key,topic_key,statement,scope,modality,fact_ids,quotes,topic_quotes,condition_groups:[{operator,source_conditions,quotes,conditions:[{statement,quotes}]}],exceptions:[{statement,quotes}]}],findings:[]}.

Topics represent recurring business capabilities, not individual headings, stories, phases or predicates. For example, eligibility and activation for the same registration process belong to one User registration topic with distinct rules; Password reset remains a separate topic. Apply this grouping from source meaning even in the first batch, so later stories can reuse the capability. The topic register is a vocabulary of existing capabilities, not an instruction to preserve an accidentally narrow heading name. Never merge scopes just to reduce the number of topics.

Represent actual atomic business prerequisites in all/any groups, including when the verified fact expresses them only in its statement. Retain any verified applicability qualifiers separately in an unspecified group; these do not replace the actual prerequisites. Prefer a single coherent rule over duplicate restatements of the same rule; multiple fact_ids can ground it. Use concise complete statements and the shortest exact quotations that retain the required meaning. Do not return explanations outside the schema.

For example, a verified condition "The user follows the email link or enters a support code" becomes one any group with that exact source_conditions entry and two elementary conditions. The corresponding all group is incorrect, and an any group with only the original compound sentence does not expose the elementary alternatives. Do not duplicate the compound phrase in an extra unspecified group. Use source_conditions=[] for a group whose fact conditions are already represented verbatim by its elementary members.

The application anchors each quotation to its unique exact occurrence before strict validation. Do not spend reasoning on counting characters for a unique quotation: use start=0,end=1 when the exact offsets are uncertain. If a quotation occurs more than once, provide the correct offsets or quote enough surrounding unchanged text to identify a unique occurrence. The application never repairs quotation text, whitespace, evidence identity or ambiguous matches.
